# From Freshwater to Seawater: Evaluating Offshore Data Centers as a Sustainable Cooling Strategy for AI Infrastructure

**Independent research by Sanket Kiran Gavali**  
**Amrita Vidyalayam · Class of 2026**

## Overview

This repository contains the manuscript and reproducibility materials for an independent scenario-based study of cooling strategies for AI data-center infrastructure.

The study compares:
1. Conventional freshwater evaporative cooling
2. Hybrid seawater-assisted cooling
3. Fully offshore submerged cooling

The analysis evaluates water use, energy efficiency, capital cost, deployment feasibility, reliability, and ecological/regulatory considerations.

## Research question

**Under what conditions could seawater-assisted or offshore cooling reduce freshwater dependence for AI infrastructure while remaining technically and economically plausible?**

## Main finding

Under the paper's stated assumptions and baseline weighting, the offshore scenario receives the highest composite score (**7.85/10**), followed by hybrid (**6.25/10**) and the freshwater baseline (**5.15/10**).

This is a conditional modeling result, not a claim that offshore data centers are universally superior.

## Method

The study uses a transparent multi-criteria decision model and includes:
- priority-weight stress testing
- hybrid/offshore break-even analysis
- input-perturbation sensitivity analysis
- comparison with published Project Natick reliability evidence

The model distinguishes published empirical evidence from analytical assumptions and scenario inputs.

## Limitations

This is a scenario-based computational study, not a full-scale physical experiment. Important uncertainties include thermal performance, lifecycle cost, corrosion/biofouling, marine heat discharge, maintenance logistics, ecological/regulatory effects, and local infrastructure conditions.
